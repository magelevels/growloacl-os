# GrowLocal competitive landscape (2026)

This is a 250-company discovery set for product strategy, positioning and partnership research. It is deliberately broader than a direct-competitor list: local businesses buy a connected stack across discovery, reputation, conversion, communication, booking, payment, retention and measurement. The CSV is the working index; the priority column identifies the first companies to study in more depth.

## What the market data says

- GetLatka reports 221 local-marketing software companies in its August 2026 industry set, with combined reported revenue of $2.2B, $1.1B in funding and 19.2K employees. Its inclusion criteria combine listings, local SEO, analytics, CRM and targeted advertising, which supports treating this as a connected category rather than a single feature market.
- G2's current local-SEO research repeatedly surfaces rank tracking, keyword research, listings, review monitoring, profile optimisation, competitor tracking and reporting as the core buyer needs.
- G2's local-listing research describes the category as a central source of truth for business information across directories, maps, social networks and store pages, often bundled with reputation, store-locator and social features.
- The four dominant product patterns are: visibility data, reputation automation, customer communication and operational conversion. Most vendors specialise in one or two of these.

## GrowLocal opportunity

The strongest positioning is not to become another listing, review or CRM dashboard. GrowLocal should own the hand-off from evidence to action:

> **From first search to repeat visit, one measurable next move.**

That promise is credible because the product already has a lead pipeline, audit evidence, proposal workflow and deadlines. The new derived next-move signal makes the promise operational: if a lead has no action, the admin workspace suggests one; if it has an action but no deadline, it asks for a date.

## Product roadmap from the scan

1. **Evidence-to-action engine (now):** derive the next move from stage and missing follow-up data; let the operator accept it in one click.
2. **Local proof ledger:** keep four lightweight measures together for each client: discovery, conversion, repeat and reputation.
3. **Owner-ready weekly brief:** turn the ledger into one priority, one owner, one deadline and one expected commercial outcome.
4. **Review-to-return loop:** connect review requests and customer follow-up to the next visit, not just star rating.
5. **Outcome attribution:** let operators record the evidence behind an improvement so future recommendations become more specific.

## Data-driven priority

The current aggregate D1 snapshot found 8 leads, 8 without a next action, 6 without a pipeline stage and 0 proposals sent. That makes follow-up momentum the highest-value product gap to address before adding more reporting surface area.

## Method and limits

The list combines current category evidence from G2 and GetLatka with established adjacent SMB workflow vendors. Priority 1 is a practical research shortlist, not a claim that every company is a direct competitor or that a public ranking proves product quality. Revenue, review counts and feature sets change; verify vendor pricing and capabilities before making commercial commitments.
